Case study

dublab mobile

A native-feeling PWA for dublab, LA's independent, community-supported radio station — built from an acquaintance's recurring complaint that after years of people saying someone should build this, as far as he knew, no one currently had.

Role

Designer, builder

Team

Solo, with Claude Code

Status

Unofficial concept, not affiliated with or endorsed by dublab

Timeline

Sept 2026, across several sessions

Platform
  • Next.js
  • PWA
  • Vercel

On a real phone — the actual point of this whole build.

01

The short version

Problem
Longtime listeners had been saying dublab needed a proper app, and as far as anyone in that conversation knew, nobody had built one.
Opportunity
Start small with an installable web app, live audio and the schedule only, real enough to send to dublab for honest feedback, then grow it once the core felt solid.
Result
Live and installable, with 5 real features and 366 real DJ profiles, used for a full day on a real home screen and sent to a contact at dublab for feedback.
02

The idea

An acquaintance who's followed dublab for years said, again, that nobody had built it an app.

One had existed years earlier, before his time; I built this without knowing that.

I started with just Tune In and Schedule; DJ and show pages came later.

03

The approach

dublab runs two feeds; its own site's source revealed the real one, Airtime Pro audio.

A Next.js route proxies the schedule and DJ data, which lack CORS headers.

Two schedule views became one schedule, not two, opening on today's current slot.

04

The bug that took real testing to catch

On real iPhones, jumping between distant days made the schedule flicker.

Four theories failed; then real Friday copy flashed mid-scroll, exposing a WebKit tile-cache bug.

The fix was architectural: show one day at a time, so there's no long scroll.

05

What got shipped / cut

Shipped: live audio with auto-reconnect, one schedule, 366 real DJs, DJ and show pages, home-screen install.

Fixed live: the flicker, a status-bar bleed, a vanishing header, and a squashed live dot.

Cut on purpose: an offline mode it couldn't honestly keep, push notifications, accounts, and user content.

Everything shipped, in one view — schedule, on-air card, and the Listen/pause toggle at its final sized-to-content width.

Trade-offs
The grain-texture overlay is a live SVG filter, not a pre-rendered static image — cheaper to build, with a small, accepted repaint cost now that it's confirmed not to be the source of the flicker bug.
Auto-reconnect retries with a capped backoff rather than indefinitely — a real dead zone should recover, but nothing hammers the stream forever if it's genuinely down.
06

Testing it for real

Frame-by-frame pixel diffs caught a black flash an early visual scan had missed.

The most useful test was listening all day, in the car, on a real phone.

Drop-outs left the player silent until you hit play, so I built auto-reconnect.

07

Outcomes

5
Real features shipped: Tune In, Schedule, DJs, Shows, About
366
Real DJs pulled from dublab's own site
1
Hard cross-device bug, root-caused and closed

Live, installable, and used for a full day on a real home screen.

Sent to a contact at dublab for honest feedback, the test it was built for.

Try it live — best experienced on a phone, added to your home screen.
